Conservation pledge

The land pays our wages.
We pay it back.

Safari tourism only works if the ecosystems do. Since 2011 we have written conservation into the fare, not the marketing: a fixed 4% of every booking goes to research partners we publish and audit annually.

  • Mara Predator Conservation Programme — collaring and conflict mitigation, funded since 2014.
  • Okavango Crane Count — annual wattled-crane census flights, our aircraft, their scientists.
  • Save the Rhino Trust Namibia — tracker salaries for two Kaokoveld patrol teams.

When to go

Read the seasons, not the brochure

Jul – OctMara crossing season. Dry grass, huge herds, cool 6 a.m. starts. Book eight months ahead — August genuinely sells out.
May – SepOkavango flood peak: the desert fills with water while the rest of Botswana dries. Best mokoro months, coldest nights.
Sep – NovNamibia shoulder season — dune light at its lowest angle, coastal fog burning off by nine, and empty roads.

How booking works

From first email to first game drive

Step one — holdTell us the departure you want. We hold seats for 10 days, no card needed, while you talk it over at home.
Step two — planA 45-minute video call with your actual lead guide — not a sales team — to tune the pace, photography priorities and dietary needs. 25% deposit confirms.
Step three — goBalance due 90 days out with your full day-by-day, packing list and insurance checklist. We meet you at arrivals holding a very small sign.
IncludedAll park & conservancy fees, light-aircraft transfers, every meal and drink in camp, laundry, and a $200,000 flying-doctor evacuation cover per guest.
Not includedInternational flights, visas, travel insurance (required, we'll check it), and gratuities — our written guide suggests $15–25 per guest per day.
