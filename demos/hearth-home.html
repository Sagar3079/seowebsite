The maker

One wheel, one kiln, no shortcuts.

I learned to throw in a community-college night class in 2016 and never really went home. Hearth & Haven began three years later in my grandfather's garage with a secondhand wheel, a kiln named Doris, and a stubborn idea: everyday dishes should be made like heirlooms.

Each firing holds about ninety pieces. From wedged clay to your kitchen shelf, a mug takes three weeks. Here is the whole honest process:

  1. 1

    Wedge

    North Carolina stoneware, kneaded until every air bubble is gone.

  2. 2

    Throw

    Shaped on the wheel — a mug takes six minutes and eight years of practice.

  3. 3

    Trim & handle

    Feet trimmed leather-hard; handles pulled by hand and attached one by one.

  4. 4

    Bisque fire

    First firing to 1830°F. Doris takes twelve hours and will not be rushed.

  5. 5

    Glaze

    Dipped in glazes I mix from my own recipes — rust, sage, and ash.

  6. 6

    Glaze fire

    To 2230°F, then two days of cooling. Opening the kiln never stops feeling like a gift.
