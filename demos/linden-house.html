A small hotel near Petworth

Nine rooms, a walled garden, and absolutely no hurry.

The Linden House is a Georgian farmhouse at the edge of the South Downs, kept by the Hale family since 2014. We do slow breakfasts, deep baths, honesty-bar evenings, and not much else — on purpose.

Garden & table

Breakfast begins in the walled garden

Half of what reaches the breakfast table is picked before you're awake. The walled garden — a third of an acre, older than the house's second wing — gives us rhubarb in April, strawberries by June, tomatoes that never see a refrigerator, and quinces that become the membrillo we serve with cheese at the honesty bar.

Eggs come from our own flock of twelve Burford Browns; the sourdough is baked each morning by Tom, who came for a weekend in 2019 and somehow never left. Bacon and sausages travel four miles from Coopers Farm in Byworth. Breakfast runs until half past ten because holidays should not have alarm clocks.

In the evenings we keep it simple: a short supper menu on Fridays and Saturdays, and the honesty bar — Sussex wines, local ales, damson gin from our own trees — every night, on the honour system, by the fire.
