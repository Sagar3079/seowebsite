The broth · a 48-hour timeline

Two days on the fire. Nine minutes in front of you.

The tonkotsu pot never really stops — each batch hands its last ladle to the next. This is what happens between deliveries.

HR 00

Bones in

90 kg of pork femur and neck bones, blanched, scrubbed, and set to a hard rolling boil.

HR 06

First skim

Foam off, fat reserved. Ginger, garlic and negi tops go in whole — they will surrender everything.

HR 24

The turn

Collagen breaks; the broth goes from cloudy to ivory. We stir every twenty minutes through the night shift.

HR 42

Tare & aroma oil

Cedar-aged soy tare and black garlic oil are finished separately — they meet the broth only in your bowl.

HR 48

Service

Strained twice, held at 92°C, and ladled at the counter. What is left at close seeds tomorrow's pot.

Queue policy

Eleven seats. Simple rules.

No reservations, ever. Put your name and party size on the chalkboard by the door and stay within earshot.

Parties of four maximum — the counter seats eleven and we will not split you across strangers' elbows.

Everyone seated orders a bowl. Sides are for sharing; broth is not.

Last bowl called 30 minutes before close. Kaedama until the final strainer is washed.

Average wait at 11pm on a Friday: 25 minutes. Worth it: always.
